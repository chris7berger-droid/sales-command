SESSION SUMMARY
---------------
Chris, at 5:51pm Pacific: "I just invoiced job number 10040 and when I filter my invoices by
date, it's not showing up, and I want to know why. This is very concerning."

He had just sent invoice #10154. The Invoices filter was set FROM 08/03/2026 TO 08/07/2026.
The invoice existed, showed SENT on the job detail's Linked Items panel, and was absent from
the list.

`invoices.sent_at` is a Postgres **date** column. Every path that marks an invoice sent wrote
`new Date().toISOString()` — a full UTC instant. Postgres casts that to the **UTC** date. At
5:51pm Pacific on Aug 7 the UTC instant is `2026-08-08T00:51Z`, so the stored date was
2026-08-08. The filter's ceiling was 08/07, and the row fell out.

This is a five-hours-a-day bug. Between 5pm Pacific and midnight, every invoice sent is stamped
with tomorrow's date, in Sales Command AND in QuickBooks. It has been in the code the whole
time and was invisible because the display path and the filter path disagreed about which
timezone they were in: `fmtD()` renders a bare date string by appending `T00:00:00` (LOCAL), so
a row stored as "2026-08-08" prints as Aug 8 — correct-looking, just a day late — while the
filter compares the raw string. Nothing ever looked broken on screen; the row just quietly
sorted and filtered as a different day.

Two more things on the same screen turned out to share the root cause, found by reading the
screenshot rather than by searching:

1. AGING READ A DAY EARLY. `Math.round((new Date() - new Date(inv.due_date)) / 86400000)`
   subtracts a LOCAL instant from a date string parsed as UTC MIDNIGHT. In Chris's screenshot
   invoice 10150, due Aug 7, showed "1d overdue" on Aug 7. 10143, due Aug 6, showed "2d". Every
   aging figure on the page was one day hot after 5pm Pacific.

2. THE PAY-APP PATH HAD THE SAME STAMP, WORSE. `send-pay-app/index.ts` runs on the edge in UTC
   — not just after 5pm Pacific but for the whole UTC-ahead window — and wrote the same
   `nowIso` into `sent_at`. `NewPayAppModal`'s printed `invoice_date` used the same
   `toISOString().slice(0,10)`, so the date on the PDF the GC receives could be tomorrow's.

DECISIONS
---------
D1. FIX THE HELPER, NOT THE CALL SITES. The tempting minimal fix was `sent_at` alone. But
    `tod()` in utils.js was the same defect in the shared helper every date comparison in the
    app already used, and CallLog carried a hand-rolled duplicate of it. Fixing `sent_at` and
    leaving a known-wrong `tod()` one file away would have left the next person to find it
    twice. The blast radius of the helper change is `over()` and Home's today-counts — both
    were ALREADY wrong in the same direction, so this corrects them rather than changing
    behavior anyone depends on.

D2. `date` COLUMNS AND timestamptz COLUMNS ARE NOT THE SAME PROBLEM, AND ONLY ONE WAS TOUCHED.
    Checked the actual `information_schema` types before editing anything. On `invoices`:
    `sent_at` and `due_date` are **date**; `paid_at`, `voided_at`, `deleted_at`,
    `viewing_token_expires_at` are **timestamptz**. `toISOString()` is CORRECT for a
    timestamptz and was left alone everywhere. Across the whole public schema, `sent_at` is a
    date on `invoices` ONLY — `proposals`, `proposal_recipients`, `invoice_recipients` all
    store timestamptz, so none of them have this bug. That check is why the fix is five files
    and not thirty.

D3. `due_date` WAS NOT TOUCHED, ON EVIDENCE. It is also a date column, but it comes from a
    manual `<input type="date">` in the new-invoice modal (`Invoices.jsx:516`), not from
    today's clock — so it never went through `toISOString()`. Confirmed against the data:
    10154's due date is 2026-09-04, which is 28 days from Aug **7**, not Aug 8. Left alone.

D4. THE IMPORT-PATH `toISOString().slice(0,10)` CALLS WERE DELIBERATELY LEFT. `importUtils.js`
    and `ArchiveImportWizard.jsx` use the same expression, but on an ALREADY-PARSED Date from a
    spreadsheet cell, not on "now". Changing those risks shifting historical import dates for
    no reported symptom. Different concern, not swept.

D5. QUICKBOOKS HAD TO BE REPAIRED TOO, AND THAT WAS NOT OBVIOUS FROM THE BUG REPORT.
    `qb-sync-invoice/index.ts:365` sets `TxnDate` from `invoice.sent_at`. Both of tonight's
    invoices were already synced (qb_invoice_id 148261 / 148262), so the wrong date had already
    left the building into the accounting system. Fixing only Sales Command would have left SC
    and QB disagreeing — worse than both being wrong the same way. Queried QB directly for
    ground truth per the standing rule rather than assuming, confirmed both at txn_date
    2026-08-08, and corrected both.

ADDENDUM 2026-08-07 — the two "not touched" items, both closed
===========================================================
Chris, on reading the close-out: "Both of these sound like issues that should be fixed."

A1. THE HISTORICAL MIS-STAMPS WERE DETECTABLE. I called them undetectable. That was wrong, and
    the correction is worth more than the fix. `invoices.sent_at` is a date and carries no time —
    but `invoices.created_at` is a **timestamptz**, and 112 of 127 sent invoices (88%) were
    created and sent in the same sitting. That makes created_at a usable witness for the send
    instant on the large majority of rows.

    THE DETECTOR (conservative — only fires where the witness is trustworthy):
      sent_at = (created_at at time zone 'UTC')::date            -- created + sent same session
      AND (created_at at time zone 'America/Los_Angeles')::date
          < (created_at at time zone 'UTC')::date                -- instant fell in the bad window
    The second line is the bug's exact signature: the UTC date is a day ahead of the Pacific
    date precisely when the instant lands between 5pm Pacific and midnight. Postgres handles
    PDT/PST, so the boundary is correctly 5pm in summer and 4pm in winter without special-casing.

    RESULT: 12 invoices, from four evening billing runs — Jun 5 (10073-10076), Jun 29
    (10097, 10098), Jul 1 (10102), Jul 13 (10119-10123). Every one created between 17:02 and
    17:42 Pacific. A tight cluster right at the boundary is itself evidence the detector is
    picking up the real mechanism and not noise.

    INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION, TWICE:
      - QB `private_memo` on 10097 reads "VOIDED/PULLED BACK 6/29/2026, 5:31:23 PM" while its
        QB txn_date said 6/30. A human-written timestamp inside QuickBooks, disagreeing with
        QuickBooks' own date field, in exactly the predicted direction.
      - The 4 invoices that DO have a real `invoice_recipients.sent_at` timestamptz
        (10051, 10088, 10089, 10124) all agree with their stored date — the detector correctly
        does NOT flag them.

    REPAIRED: all 12 in Supabase (`sent_at` set to the Pacific date of created_at). 9 of them in
    QuickBooks. The other 3 (10097, 10098, 10102) are voided $0 test invoices — deliberately not
    touched in QB; editing a voided transaction is pointless and the SC-side value is cosmetic.
    NO MONTH BOUNDARIES WERE CROSSED by any of the 12 (Jun 5→6, Jun 29→30, Jul 1→2, Jul 13→14),
    so no QB reporting period moved and no P&L or A/R aging total changed. That was checked
    BEFORE writing, and it is the reason this was safe to do without an accountant in the loop.
    Post-repair detector re-run: 0 remaining.

    RESIDUAL, GENUINELY UNKNOWN: 15 invoices where `sent_at` differs from created_at's UTC date
    — i.e. created one day, sent another. The witness doesn't apply and nothing else records the
    send instant. Any of them sent after 5pm is still a day late. This is the honest floor of
    what's knowable from current data. The durable fix is to record a real send TIMESTAMP, not
    just a date — see POINTERS #5.

A2. THE `supabase/.temp` NOISE WAS NOT WHERE I SAID IT WAS. I recommended
    `git rm -r --cached supabase/.temp` on main. On main that is a **no-op** — main dropped
    those files back in `b112470` (migration consolidation) and tracks zero of them. The noise

  5. RECORD A SEND INSTANT, NOT A SEND DATE. The 15 unresolvable invoices exist because nothing
     durably stores WHEN an invoice was sent — `sent_at` is a date, and `invoice_recipients.sent_at`
     (a timestamptz, the right shape) is populated on only 4 of 127. Either backfill that path so
     every send writes it, or add `invoices.sent_instant timestamptz`. Until then this class of
     question stays unanswerable after the fact. Migration lives in command-suite-db.
  6. THE GENERAL LESSON, AND IT IS THE POINT OF THIS ADDENDUM: "undetectable" was a conclusion
     reached from ONE column instead of from the schema. `created_at` was sitting right there,
     already in a query result printed earlier in the same session. Before declaring data
     unrecoverable, enumerate every timestamptz on the table and every related table — a date
     column loses information, but the rows around it usually haven't.
